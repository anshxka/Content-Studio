Subject line: Why running ads is not marketing (Issue #1)

Most people think marketing is just making pretty Instagram posts and burning budget on Meta ads. I used to think the same when I started.

Picture this: You just launched a brand new energy drink. You set up a flashy campaign on Instagram, target 18-to-24-year-olds, and spend all your pocket money. Two weeks later, crickets. Zero sales.

Why? Because you pushed an ad without understanding what marketing actually is.

This week's lesson · Core marketing

What marketing actually is: needs, value and exchange

When you are new to the marketing industry, it is very easy to confuse the tools with the discipline. You see people talking about ROAS (Return on Ad Spend, which means how much revenue you make for every rupee you spend on ads), CPC (Cost Per Click), and CTR (Click-Through Rate). These are tactics. They are not marketing.

At its core, marketing is about three fundamental pillars: Needs, Value, and Exchange.

Let's break these down over a cup of chai.

A **need** is a state of felt deprivation. If you are sitting at your desk studying late at night and you feel hungry, that is a need. Food satisfies it. A **want** is the specific form that need takes shaped by culture and personality—you don't just want food, you want a cheesy pizza.

**Value** is the consumer's estimation of a product's capacity to satisfy their set of needs. When Zomato delivers that pizza hot within 20 minutes while you are exhausted, the value isn't just the dough and cheese; the value is speed, convenience, and saving you from cooking.

Finally, an **exchange** is the act of obtaining a desired object from someone by offering something in return. You give Zomato ₹400, and Zomato gives you the pizza and peace of mind. Marketing happens right in the middle of these three concepts.

Let’s look at an everyday Indian example: Paper Boat. When Paper Boat launched traditional drinks like Aam Panna and Jaljeera in colorful pouches, they didn't just sell a beverage. They understood a deep consumer need: nostalgia. People missed their grandmother's summer drinks. Paper Boat created value by packaging childhood memories, and consumers willingly paid for that emotional exchange.

Common Beginner Mistakes

1. **Falling in love with the product instead of the problem:** Beginners often focus on how cool their design or ad copy is, ignoring whether anyone actually cares about the problem it solves.
2. **Treating ads as magic:** Thinking that turning on a Google Search ad will fix a bad product or a broken business model.
3. **Ignoring the target audience's reality:** Building marketing messaging based on what *you* think is cool, rather than what your consumer's actual daily struggles are.

How This Shows Up in a Real Marketing Job

When you land your first role—whether as a performance marketing intern or a social media executive—your seniors won't just ask you to write ad captions. They will ask: 'What is the consumer pain point we are solving here?' When you write copy or set up targeting parameters on Meta Ads Manager or Google Ads, you aren't just clicking buttons. You are crafting a message of value to bridge a gap between a customer's need and your brand's solution.

    Quick commerce (ultra-fast delivery apps like Blinkit and Zepto) and Gen Z consumers are driving massive growth for Direct-to-Consumer brands beyond big cities.   3. DigiPlus Fest 2026: How Kotak is scaling hyper-personalisation from consumer insights to experiences
    Kotak is focusing on hyper-personalization by turning raw consumer insights into custom experiences. Beginners should understand that data isn't just numbers on a spreadsheet; it represents real human behaviors that can be used to make marketing feel personal rather than spammy.
  4. Test before you scale: WPP Media's new playbook for brand launches
    WPP Media released a playbook emphasizing testing strategies before scaling budgets on new product launches. As a marketer, this teaches you the importance of running small-scale experiments (A/B testing) before wasting massive amounts of company money on untested ideas.
  5. AI hiring jumps over 20% as India's white-collar jobs market turns more selective
    The corporate hiring market is becoming highly selective, with AI-related job roles surging by over 20%. Freshers must adapt by learning how to use AI tools (like ChatGPT or Looker Studio) as copilots to boost their daily productivity and stay competitive.

Career corner

Since campus hiring is shifting heavily toward practical skills and job readiness, stop relying solely on your college degree or certification certificates. Pick one tool mentioned in this newsletter (like Meta Ads, Google Ads, or SQL), spend 30 minutes a day building a small mock project, and document your learnings on LinkedIn. Employers value proof of execution over empty promises.

Your 15-minute homework
