This week's lesson · Brand marketing

Brand identity, voice and consistency

When you are new to marketing, it is very easy to get obsessed with performance metrics—clicks, conversions, ROAS (Return on Ad Spend: the revenue a business earns for every dollar it spends on advertising). But long before a customer clicks your ad, they need to recognize who you are. That brings us to the holy trinity of branding: identity, voice, and consistency.

Let’s break these down simply. Your **brand identity** is the visual fingerprint of your company. It is your logo, your color palette, your typography, and your imagery. Think of Zomato's distinct red color and cheeky, witty notifications. If Zomato suddenly started using corporate blue and boring, formal text, you would wonder if their account was hacked.

Your **brand voice** is how your brand speaks. Is it a witty friend who cracks jokes on Twitter? Or is it a calm, reassuring mentor? Voice determines your vocabulary, sentence length, and tone. If a luxury hotel brand started using internet slang like 'no cap' or 'fr fr' in its email newsletter, it would immediately break the illusion of luxury.

Finally, we have **consistency**. This is where most beginners (and even large companies) drop the ball. Consistency means ensuring that your visual identity and brand voice remain the same across every single touchpoint—whether a user sees your ad on Instagram, reads an article on your blog, chats with your support bot, or walks into a physical store.

Let’s look at an everyday example: Amul. For decades, Amul has maintained the exact same brand voice—witty, topical, and tied to current affairs—using the iconic Amul girl illustration. Their visual identity and tone of voice have stayed remarkably consistent through generations. When you see an Amul topical ad, you instantly know who made it without even looking at the logo.

Here are some common beginner mistakes to avoid:
1. **Chasing every aesthetic trend:** Just because a chaotic, neon-heavy design style is viral on TikTok this week doesn't mean your corporate finance client should use it.
2. **Inconsistent tone across channels:** Writing formal, robotic copy on LinkedIn while trying to be a meme lord on Instagram creates brand confusion.
3. **Changing brand guidelines too often:** Rebranding every six months confuses your audience and destroys whatever trust you built up.

How does this show up in a real marketing job? Whether you are writing a Meta ad caption, scheduling a tweet, or setting up a Google Display banner, you are the guardian of the brand. Performance marketers often want to swap out creative assets every single day to fight ad fatigue. Your job is to keep testing new angles while ensuring the core brand identity and voice never get lost in the noise.

  1. Airtel overtakes HDFC Bank to become India's most valuable brand; Meesho tops risers
    Airtel surpassed HDFC Bank to claim the top spot in brand value, while Meesho saw massive upward movement. Beginners should learn that long-term brand building and massive market reach directly translate into financial and valuation power, way beyond short-term sales.
  2. Coca-Cola pivots from divisive AI-generated ads for holiday campaign
    Coca-Cola shifted away from fully AI-generated holiday ads after facing mixed public reactions. This teaches marketers that while AI tools are great for speed, relying entirely on machine-generated creative can strip away the human emotional resonance that a strong brand needs.

    Wella partnered with celebrity Manushi Chhillar to launch an identity-focused campaign centered on self-expression. Beginners should note how brands use high-profile ambassadors not just for reach, but to anchor specific personality traits to their product line.

Career corner

When building your resume or portfolio as a beginner, don't just list the tools you know. Show how you maintained brand consistency across campaigns. If you designed ad creatives or wrote copy, include examples that prove you understood the brand's voice and didn't just throw random templates together. Hiring managers look for candidates who understand strategic alignment, not just button-pushing.

Your 15-minute homework

Pick three of your favorite consumer brands on Instagram. Look at their last nine grid posts and read their recent captions. Write down a 3-sentence description of their 'brand voice' (e.g., sarcastic friend, calm expert, high-energy hype person). If a stranger read their captions out loud without showing the logo, would you still know who they are?
