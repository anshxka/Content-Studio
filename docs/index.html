This week's lesson · Core marketing

Consumer Behaviour: How People Really Decide What to Buy

When beginners enter marketing, they often assume buyers behave like robots: compare features, calculate costs, and pick the most rational product. In reality, human beings are emotional, distracted, and overwhelmed by choices. We don't buy logically; we buy emotionally and justify it logically afterward.

What is consumer behaviour?

Consumer behaviour is the study of how people discover, evaluate, purchase, and use products. It combines psychology, sociology, and economics to explain why someone picks Brand A over Brand B when both do the exact same thing.

To understand this, you need to know Daniel Kahneman’s concept of System 1 and System 2 thinking:
- **System 1 (Fast & Emotional):** Automatic, unconscious, and energy-saving. When you crave an iced latte on a hot afternoon, that's System 1.
- **System 2 (Slow & Logical):** Deliberate, analytical, and calculating. When you compare interest rates on personal loans, that's System 2.

Most everyday consumer purchases happen in System 1. If your marketing forces people to think too hard, you lose them.

The everyday example: Zomato's 7:30 PM nudge

Look at how food delivery apps like Zomato trigger purchases. They don't send you a push notification saying, *"Our platform connects you to 4,200 vetted commercial kitchens with an average prep latency of 14 minutes."* That would appeal to System 2, and nobody cares.

Instead, on a rainy Friday evening, you get: *"Barish + hot piping momos? Say no more."*

They target your context, your state of mind, and an existing craving. They reduce cognitive load (the mental effort required to make a decision) and make the choice effortless. By the time your logical brain asks, *"Should I spend money on takeout?"*, your thumb is already on the checkout screen.

Beginner mistakes to watch out for

1. **Feature dumping:** Listing 20 product specifications in an ad instead of explaining the transformation or feeling the product gives the buyer.
2. **Ignoring friction:** Friction is anything that slows a user down—confusing forms, forced logins, or hidden shipping costs. Even high intent evaporates when checkout feels like homework.
3. **Designing for yourself instead of the user:** Assuming your target customer cares about what you care about. A Gen Z shopper values speed and community validation; a procurement manager values risk reduction and compliance.

How this shows up in your day job

Whether you are writing copy, running ads, or setting up automated push notifications, consumer behaviour is your foundation:

- **In Paid Ads (Meta/Google Ads):** You don't just test blue buttons versus green buttons. You test behavioural angles—social proof (reviews), loss aversion (fear of missing out), or convenience.
- **In CRM & Retention (CleverTap, Push Notifications):** You map notifications to natural user habits (e.g., sending replenishment reminders right when someone is about to run out of protein powder).
- **In Landing Pages:** You arrange information to answer the buyer's hidden anxiety first: *"Can I trust this brand? Will this work for me? What if I want a refund?"*

Great marketing never forces people to do something unnatural. It identifies what they already desire and removes the hurdles in their way.

With LinkedIn actively penalizing low-effort 'AI slop' and recruiters drowning in generic AI-generated resumes, standard templates will get you ignored. Your 15-minute homework

Pick the last non-essential item you bought online. Open a notes app and write down: 1) What triggered the initial desire (an ad, an emotion, boredom)? 2) Was it System 1 (fast/impulse) or System 2 (comparison/research)? 3) What was one piece of friction that almost made you drop off? This simple teardown will change how you view your own funnels.
