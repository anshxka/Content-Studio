Subject line: Why your customers aren’t following the traditional funnel

Picture this: You spend weeks building a pristine marketing funnel—Awareness, Consideration, Conversion—only to watch users drop off in mysterious ways.

They don't move in a straight line. They bounce between Instagram, YouTube reviews, Google searches, and WhatsApp chats before ever buying anything.

If you're wondering why your textbook marketing strategies feel broken in the real world, you aren't doing it wrong. The funnel has a massive glitch.

This week's lesson · Core marketing

The marketing funnel vs the messy middle

For decades, the marketing funnel has been the holy grail of advertising. You run an ad to build awareness, the user considers your product, and finally, they convert. Clean, linear, and predictable. But if you have spent even five minutes looking at real user behavior, you know this model is largely dead.

Consumers today don't walk down a straight line. They enter what Google calls the 'messy middle'—an infinite loop of exploration and evaluation. A shopper might see an Instagram reel about a skincare product, search for Reddit reviews, ask a friend on WhatsApp, watch a YouTube comparison video, check prices on Amazon, and then finally buy it three weeks later. Sometimes, they loop back to exploration even after deciding to buy.

What is the Messy Middle?

The messy middle sits right between the trigger (what sparks the need) and the purchase. It is a cognitive space where consumers manage an overwhelming amount of information. Instead of narrowing down choices like the traditional funnel suggests, consumers actually expand their options during this phase, exploring new brands they have never heard of before.

Let’s look at an everyday example. Imagine you want to buy a new water purifier for your home. Your trigger is moving into a new apartment. Instead of seeing an ad and instantly buying Brand A, you enter the loop. You search Google for best purifiers, read consumer forums, watch tech reviewers on YouTube, look up Instagram ads of D2C challengers, and compare warranty terms. You are expanding your consideration set, not narrowing it.

Common Beginner Mistakes

1. Treating channels in silos: Beginners often think Meta ads are only for 'top-of-funnel' and Google Search is only for 'bottom-of-funnel'. In reality, users see your search ad, go to Instagram to vet your vibe, and then convert via a retargeting campaign.
2. Ignoring the information clutter: Trying to force a hard sell when the user is stuck in the exploration loop. Instead of pushing discounts, you need to provide helpful content, reviews, and trust signals.
3. Expecting linear attribution: Believing that the last ad a user clicked gets 100% of the credit for the sale. Attribution models are messy because the user journey is messy.

How This Shows Up in a Real Marketing Job

When you land your first performance marketing or brand role, you will quickly realize that dashboards don't tell the whole story. Your manager won't just ask about click-through rates; they will ask how different touchpoints influence each other. You will learn to build 'always-on' marketing strategies that capture users wherever they are in their chaotic loop—ensuring your brand has enough reviews, creator videos, and search visibility to win the messy middle.
